# eMudhra intermediate CA incident (October 2026)

This page records the fixed identifiers used by Certainted's built-in incident watchlist. On 17 October 2026, a researcher reported that the private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CAs were recoverable. eMudhra revoked its eight certificates on 18 October with reason `caCompromise`; Sectigo revoked eight cross-signed certificates carrying the same keys on 19 October. Six keys belong to TLS-issuing CAs and two to code-signing CAs. The 16 certificate fingerprints below therefore represent **eight distinct compromised CA public keys**, not 16 separate key compromises. [eMudhra's incident statement](https://emudhra.com/en/post/certificate-security-incident), [Mozilla's incident record](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449), and [Sectigo's advisory](https://www.sectigo.com/resource-library/advisory-on-compromised-emudhra-intermediate-certificates) document the scope and revocations.

## Affected certificate identities

`Certificate SHA-256` identifies one exact certificate. `SPKI SHA-256` identifies its public key and is shared by the eMudhra-issued certificate and its Sectigo cross-sign. The serial must always be interpreted together with the issuer; serial numbers alone are not globally unique. The identifiers match [Mozilla's certificate and serial list](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449), [the incident's certificate manifest](https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/impacted-intermediates.json), and [Go's eight-key distrust list](https://go.dev/src/crypto/x509/verify.go).

## What the built-in watch means

The bundled identifiers are **incident-derived watchlist data** with the linked source record. They are not a CRL fetched or signature-verified by this Certainted instance, and they do not replace a current revocation check. Certainted makes no automatic network request to update the list. Importing a signed CRL through the separate CRL feature produces a distinct, verified-CRL finding and retains its own issuer, signature, and freshness evidence.

An independent TLS probe that presents one of these exact certificates or public keys is a **potential exposure finding at that probe's time and network vantage**. It does not prove the original application received the same chain, accepted it, or was intercepted. Certainted does not terminate application TLS or block connections. The two code-signing CAs are included for incident completeness, but they are not TLS issuers and should not be treated as valid TLS trust paths.

The upstream `emSign ECC Root CA - G3` was **distrusted as a precaution**, including by [Mozilla](https://groups.google.com/a/mozilla.org/g/dev-security-policy/c/0R3XLJL6FuE/m/Kp1v6CPuAAAJ) and [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149555). Its own private key was not reported compromised. `AAA Certificate Services` cross-signed the affected intermediate keys; its key was not reported compromised either. Neither root is labeled a compromised intermediate by this list. eMudhra says its eight affected intermediates issued no legitimate end-entity certificates; that statement does not rule out forged certificates created by others with the recoverable keys.
